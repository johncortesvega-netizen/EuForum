# EU Hard-Boundary Map + Sydney Protocol THRESHOLD Layer

Patch 11 separates two routes that must not be confused.

## 1. EU hard-boundary floor

European legal and human-rights categories define the hard floor for direct harm and illegal-content review. These categories can route to accountable human moderation, temporary visibility limits, removal, escalation, and appeal receipts where appropriate.

Mapped categories:

- child sexual abuse or exploitation;
- terrorist content or violent extremism;
- direct threat or incitement to violence;
- doxxing or private personal-data exposure;
- illegal hate speech under applicable law;
- targeted harassment campaign;
- illegal harmful instructions;
- spam or bot flooding;
- severe safety emergency.

These are not automatic filters in this prototype. They are policy categories for human review.

## 2. Sydney Protocol THRESHOLD layer

Everything outside the hard-boundary floor remains in the Sydney Protocol THRESHOLD clarification layer unless human moderators determine otherwise under the hard-boundary policy.

THRESHOLD examples:

- strong political claim without evidence;
- corruption-risk language without sources;
- propaganda-like framing;
- pressure language;
- authority overclaim;
- mechanism gap;
- translation caveat;
- dignity-risk wording that does not meet hard-boundary criteria;
- escalation risk;
- repair question needed.

The Sydney Protocol may surface pressure, missing evidence, translation caveats, dignity risk, escalation risk, and repair questions.

The Sydney Protocol must not judge, rank, punish, censor, decide truth, decide guilt, decide corruption, or enforce visibility actions.

## Core rule

> European law and human-rights categories define the hard floor. The Sydney Protocol handles THRESHOLD pressure through clarification only.

## Receipt rule

Hard-boundary actions require human accountability receipts. Sydney Protocol THRESHOLD prompts create clarification receipts only.
